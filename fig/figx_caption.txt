Figure X. Lateral OFC represents reward identity during conditioning.

(A) Cue-evoked odor prediction during conditioning. Illustrative food-odor trials show directly conditioned cues (B1/B2), prediction of their associated odors, and subsequent odor delivery. Dashed arrows link cues to their expected odors; solid arrows indicate subsequent odor delivery. Dashed boxes depict expected outcomes, not odor images displayed during the task; solid boxes indicate delivered odors. The intervening delay is omitted. The bracket groups the cue and prediction stages targeted by the analyses. Cue and odor assignments follow the illustrative mapping in Figure 1 and were counterbalanced across participants. Odor-delivery events for both food odors and clean air were included as one pooled nuisance regressor in the single-trial cue GLMs, modeling delivery separately from cue-evoked responses. (B) Conditioning results. Left plot: odor-identity RSA. Spearman correlations between neural and expected odor-identity RDMs were computed from single-trial estimates spanning the cue and prediction period (0-5 s after cue onset; EXT5s), retaining only comparisons between presentations from different runs. Right plot: cross-task decoding. A linear support vector machine trained to distinguish the two food odors in the independent passive localizer was tested on early conditioning cue estimates (0-2 s; EARLY). Balanced accuracy averages classification accuracy across the two odor identities. Both analyses used correct food-odor trials pooled across all available conditioning runs in the same participants (n = 29; 28 contributed three runs and one contributed runs 1-2). The independently defined bilateral OFC regions are the same as in Figure 3. Points represent individual participants, and open diamonds show means with t-based 95% confidence intervals. Dashed lines indicate zero RSA correlation (left) and 50% chance accuracy (right). Asterisks denote uncorrected p < 0.05 from one-sided one-sample t tests against these respective baselines; n.s., not significant.
